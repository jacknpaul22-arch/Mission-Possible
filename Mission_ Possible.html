Space Mission Design Game. Callsign:
How to play
  1. Pick a mission and read its goal.
  2. Build your spacecraft in the five tabs. Watch the readiness meters change live.
  3. Launch. Random events need your call, and each choice has a cost.
  4. Your score comes from science returned, budget left and safety margins.
Data and sources

This is a teaching model, not a flight simulator. Here is what is grounded in NASA material and what is simplified for play.

Based on NASA material
  • Mars orbits at about 1.52 AU and receives about 586 W/m² of sunlight, roughly 43% of Earth's. The power model uses this (NASA Mars fact sheet).
  • Mars to Earth radio signals take about 3 to 22 minutes one way, worked out from distances in the same fact sheet.
  • Jupiter gets about 25 times less sunlight than Earth (NASA/JPL Juno).
  • The real missions in the "What we learned" cards: Mars Climate Orbiter, Galileo's antenna, Voyager 1's thrusters.
  • The rocket equation and inverse-square sunlight are standard physics.
Simplified or invented for gameplay
  • Rocket capacities, prices and reliability, and instrument masses, power, data rates and science values, are generic game values, not specific flight hardware.
  • Delta-v targets are rounded estimates, budgets are in game dollars, and the power and downlink formulas are simplified.
  • Flight events and their odds are invented to teach trade-offs.
  • The mission findings and the mission team are fictional.
